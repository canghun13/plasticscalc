Runner Volume Calculator

Calculate the combined cold-runner volume from full-round and trapezoidal segment groups. Enter drawing dimensions in mm or in, count repeated physical segments, and keep the volume breakdown with your material record.

Formula and method

Full round: A = πd² / 4
Trapezoid: A = (B + b)h / 2
Group volume = A × L × quantity
Total runner volume = sum of group volumes.

d is the complete diameter, B and b are the parallel wide and narrow bases, h is the perpendicular cross-section height, and L is one physical segment's centerline length. With mm inputs, divide mm³ by 1,000 for cm³. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm; 1 in³ = 16.387064 cm³.

Inputs and units

Use CAD or controlled mold drawings. Group only segments with identical shape, dimensions and length. Every dimension and quantity must be positive; quantity is a whole-number count. New groups start blank except for quantity 1. Choose mm or in for all dimensions; switching units converts entered dimensions, including temporarily hidden shape fields. Calculate after editing to update the result.

Worked example

The retained default, one full-round 6 mm diameter segment of 800 mm, gives π × 6² / 4 × 800 ÷ 1,000 = 22.62 cm³.

A trapezoid with bases 8 and 4 mm, perpendicular height 3 mm, length 100 mm and quantity 4 adds 7.2 cm³. Combined with the default round group: 29.82 cm³. The unrounded values are summed before display rounding.

Count physical geometry once

Use the number of identical segments, not the number of cavities. A common main runner is entered once; four identical branch runners can be one group with quantity 4. Do not enter a whole-network length and its individual branches together. Different lengths or cross-sections need separate groups.

Read the trapezoid correctly

Enter the two parallel bases and the perpendicular section height, not the sloping wall length. Wide base must be at least the narrow base; equal bases give the rectangular limiting case. This arithmetic uses the entered ideal section, not a guessed width, inscribed-circle diameter, angle rule or recommended runner size.

Reconcile against the CAD volume

Each group is a constant-section prism or cylinder. Rounded trapezoid corners, tapered or varying runners, intersections, sharp bends and overlapping junctions can change the actual solid volume. Compare with the CAD solid or a measured cooled runner before using the estimate in a quote or process record. Do not disguise a taper by entering its average diameter.

Interpretation, assumptions, and limitations

This is a geometric material-accounting estimate. It does not calculate pressure drop, flow balance, shear, cooling time, polymer behavior, or an acceptable runner diameter. Sprue, gates, wells, cavities and hot-runner manifolds are excluded. The 20-group limit is an interface limit, not an engineering sizing rule. Invalid or out-of-range arithmetic clears the old result. Processing and Copy happen in this browser tab; there is no upload, storage or persistence.

FAQ

Should I add a sprue or density here?

No. Calculate the separate tapered Sprue Volume and add it once only if the downstream record needs runner plus sprue. Use the appropriate included volume with Runner Weight and grade-specific solid density for cooled mass. Do not substitute a geometric volume for a measured complete shot.

Does changing mm to in change the mold?

No. The dimensions are physically converted, not relabeled. Recalculate after switching; the displayed cm³ and in³ should describe the same geometry within floating-point and display rounding.
