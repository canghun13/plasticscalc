Scrap Rate Calculator

Calculate scrap rate from good plus scrapped parts, or total produced plus scrapped parts. Combine non-overlapping runs by their actual production counts, review each run, and copy the count record.

Formula and method

Scrap rate = scrap ÷ (good + scrap) × 100.

In Total + scrap mode, total already includes scrap: good = total − scrap. Across runs, combined scrap rate = sum of scrapped parts ÷ sum of total produced × 100. Do not average run percentages unless the production counts are equal.

Inputs and units

Enter whole-part counts from the same part, process boundary, and inspection rule. Choose Good + scrap when your record counts accepted parts separately. Choose Total + scrap when the production counter already includes scrapped parts. Switching basis converts a valid row without changing its population. A new run starts blank and must be completed or removed. Use up to 20 non-overlapping runs, each with positive production; zero scrap and all-scrap runs are valid.

Worked example

300 scrap ÷ (9,700 good + 300 scrap) × 100 = 3%, with 10,000 total and 97% good-part yield. The same record in Total + scrap mode is 10,000 total and 300 scrap.

For unequal runs, 10 scrap from 100 total (10%) plus 9 scrap from 900 total (1%) gives 19 ÷ 1,000 = 1.9% combined, not the 5.5% simple average.

Match the counter to the denominator

A machine production counter may already include the rejected parts. Do not put that total into the Good parts field: 300 scrap and 10,000 total means 3%, not 300 ÷ 10,300. Reconcile good + scrap = total against the run record before carrying a rate into planning.

Pool counts, preserve each run

Add runs only when they use the same part and accounting definition. Do not enter a shift subtotal and its component batches together. The breakdown keeps each run visible; a combined percentage can conceal a bad small run. A count-weighted part rate is not a material-mass loss rate and must not combine unlike parts as a mass estimate.

Separate scrap from rework and recovered material

This two-category record assumes each produced part is either good or scrapped under your stated reporting rule. If rework or uninspected parts remain separate, classify and reconcile them before using good-part yield. Yield here is not automatically first-pass yield or OEE Quality. Recovered runners, purge, and kg of regrind are material flows, not additional scrapped-part counts.

Interpretation, assumptions, and limitations

The result describes the entered population; it does not diagnose defects, prescribe an acceptable scrap benchmark, estimate costs, or certify process quality. Use consistent final disposition and period boundaries. Counts and their combined sum must be within JavaScript's exact whole-number range (up to 9,007,199,254,740,991). Inputs are processed in this tab, not uploaded or saved; Reset restores the example.

FAQ

Why is the combined rate different from the average of shifts?

Runs with more produced parts contribute more to the combined result. Add scrap counts and production counts first, then divide. Keep the per-run breakdown to investigate variation.

Which percentage should I use next?

Use scrap rate with Scrap-Adjusted Output only when its population matches your production plan. Use the Scrap and Regrind Guide for the separate material-recovery decision. Do not substitute this part-count rate for kg loss or raw-material purchase allowance.
